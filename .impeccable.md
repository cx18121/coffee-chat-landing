# Necto — Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Finance students (juniors/seniors) at target schools — Wharton, Stern, Ross, Georgetown, Notre Dame — actively recruiting for IB, PE, VC, and AM roles. They're ambitious, time-constrained, and highly competitive. They're using Necto during recruiting season when every week counts. The job to be done: land more coffee chats with less manual effort so they can focus on the conversations, not the logistics.

### Brand Personality
**Bold · Modern · Aspirational**

Necto is the tool serious finance students use to get ahead. It should feel like a premium consumer product built specifically for their world — not another generic startup tool. The voice is confident without being arrogant, modern without being gimmicky.

### Emotional Goals (First Impression)
A visitor landing for the first time should feel *all four* simultaneously:
- **"This is going to change my recruiting"** — excitement and genuine aha
- **"Finally, someone built this"** — relief that a felt pain is solved
- **"I need to get on this waitlist now"** — FOMO and urgency
- **"This looks legit and polished"** — immediate trust and credibility

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Reference**: Superhuman — soft periwinkle/lavender-to-sky gradient, premium serif headlines, glassmorphism UI cards, airy whitespace, consumer-grade polish
- **Palette**: Periwinkle/lavender gradient hero (`#7570C0` → `#EBF2FB`), near-black dark (`#13101E`), white/glass surfaces off the hero
- **Typography**: Fraunces (display serif, italic for emotional emphasis) + DM Sans (clean, modern body)
- **Surfaces**: Glassmorphism cards with `backdrop-filter: blur`, layered shadows, white-glass borders. Off-hero sections use clean white / `#F9F9FC`

### Anti-References (What Necto Must NOT Look Like)
- ❌ **Generic SaaS template** — no purple gradient on white, no "Hero + 3 feature icons" in Inter
- ❌ **Dark/developer tool** — no dark mode, no terminal vibes, no Vercel/Linear/Raycast aesthetic
- ❌ **Corporate/enterprise** — no stiff, buttoned-up, bank-website energy
- ❌ **Overly playful/consumer** — not Robinhood-bright, not TikTok-fun; finance context requires gravitas

### Scope
Landing page now, full product app eventually. The design system (tokens, components, typography scale) should be extensible to a dashboard and product UI. Keep CSS variables clean and semantic — they'll become a real design system.

### Design Principles

1. **Premium by restraint** — Elegance comes from what's left out. Use generous whitespace, a limited palette, and Fraunces sparingly to signal quality.

2. **Airy confidence** — The periwinkle gradient is the signature. Every section should feel light, soft, and sure of itself — never heavy, never cluttered.

3. **Real product, not a brochure** — UI mockups and product screenshots should look like a real, buildable product. The cards, tables, and email composers establish credibility before the product exists.

4. **Finance context earns trust** — Specific details (firm names, deal references, school badges) outperform generic copy. Design should amplify specificity, not obscure it.

5. **Extensible toward a design system** — CSS variables, semantic class names, and component patterns should be clean enough to promote into a full app design system without a rewrite.
